## Who we are

We're artists and builders and people who are good with their hands, plus a
few whose entire skill set is being willing to carry heavy things at 2am. A
few times a year we pool our money and build a place for people to land.

Nobody gets paid. There's no company behind this and nobody who owns it. Just
whoever volunteers to lead the next one, and the crew who shows up to build it
with them. A production tech at a festival once asked what we were making to
run our stage. We told him we'd paid to be there.

---

## What we do

**By day it's a lounge.** Hammocks, carpets, shade, open sky, all of it
floating in the Jellyfish soundscape. Somewhere to land between sets, or to
disappear for six hours if that's the kind of day you're having.

**After dark it turns into something else entirely.** Jellyfish hang lit from
the inside, breathing. Lasers cut the haze. LEDs pulse until the whole room
seems to move with you. There's real flooring underfoot, because we know
exactly what you intend to do until sunrise and we've seen what concrete does
to people's knees. Outside, speaker stacks, uplit trees, and fire pits for
anyone who wants to stay in the music without being swallowed by it.

The sound is the real thing. Commercial rig, run by people who do this
professionally. It's the one place we refuse to cut corners. Shelly, our art
car, syncs straight to the main system, so the sound stays whole no matter how
far you drift.

Residents and guests set the tone about half the time. The rest of it stays
open for whoever wanders up with a USB and something to prove.

## Do what you're good at

Nobody here gets handed a job description.

The camp runs on whoever steps forward, which means you end up doing the thing
you actually want to do. Like building? You'll be up a ladder rigging fabric.
Handy with a needle? You'll be making jellyfish that hundreds of people walk
under. Cook, and the kitchen is yours and everyone will love you for it. Play
music, and you'll get a slot. Whatever your thing is, including the strange
one you've never had an excuse to try, the answer here is usually just "cool,
go do it."

That last part is the whole point. If you want to run an activation, you can
run an activation. Members have proposed a pop-up izakaya, a dating game on our
main stage, an art car. All of them happened, because somebody said "I want to
build this" and the camp said "great, what do you need."

Some of them worked beautifully. Some were glorious disasters. Everything we
build is a gift, and if a gift lands badly, we learn from it and build the
next one better. Nobody's getting fired for trying something weird.

You don't need permission. You need an idea and enough stubbornness to see it
through.

## Nobody is paying us

Not the festivals, not a brand, not anybody. We pay our own way in, then spend
the weekend working.

That's not a complaint, it's a design choice, and it protects the only thing
we actually care about. No sponsor gets to put a logo on the jellyfish. No
brand tells us what the room should look like or what the sound should be.
Nobody's optimizing our stage for content. Every decision about what we build
gets made by the people building it, for the people who'll be standing in it
at 4am.

We're doing this for the love of the game, and it shows in the room.

## Where we came from

Most of us met over a decade ago at meetups and shows. Strangers who kept
turning up on the same dancefloors until we weren't strangers anymore.

The first build was a renegade. No placement, no budget, no permission. Just a
pile of pooled cash and a group of people who wanted to find out what they
could put up. It worked well enough that we did it again. Then somewhere else.
Then with a real footprint and a real stage and a line of people waiting to
get in.

Every year it gets more ambitious, and every year the crew gets better at
pulling it off.

## For festivals

We show up self contained. Our own structure, sound, lighting, power, and
crew. One day to build, half a day to strike, leave no trace, nothing left
behind but a flat patch of ground.

Everything moves in a rented van and gets staged by hand. No forklifts, no
heavy equipment, no demands on your team. Safety Guardians on site throughout,
and the electrical run by people qualified to run it.

## Coming aboard

No application. No fee. No vetting committee. Most people arrive because they
know somebody, or because they wandered in at 3am and came back the next
afternoon to ask how any of it got there.

Fair warning: everyone builds, everyone takes a shift, everyone helps tear it
all down. That's not a rule anyone enforces. It's the reason there's anything
to show up to.
