## The shape of the thing

The Jellyfish Collective is decentralized and fluid on purpose. There is no
fixed long-term hierarchy, no permanent board, and no static direction. It
operates strictly project by project, on a voluntary basis.

That's not disorganization. It's a deliberate choice that keeps the collective
from calcifying into the thing it was built to avoid.

---

## Roles

**Camp Lead.** For each activation, one or more people step up as Camp Lead.
They control and manage that singular event. Their authority begins when
planning starts and ends when the last truck is unloaded back home.

**Lead positions.** Leads own a domain and pick a team of jellies to help them
deliver it:

- **Production BOH (Tech)** — plans and operates the activation itself: sound
  system, lights, stage design, lasers, music, DJs, and everything that makes
  it run. Also responsible for making sure it's staffed.
- **Production FOH (Deco)** — the decor and the vibe of the activation and the
  private lounge. Pre-event creation and acquisition, plus everything we
  already own — LEDs, jellyfish, lighting. Also keeps communal camp
  infrastructure safe and stable.
- **Art Car** — Shelly.
- **Artist Relations** — resident and guest DJs, and coordination with
  festival artists dropping in.

**Officer positions.** Officers work with the whole camp rather than running a
team:

- **Financial** — dues collection, budget, expense tracking, reimbursements.
- **Logistics** — transport, storage, staging, load-in and load-out.

**Jellies.** Everyone else. Which is most of us, most of the time.

Leads assign shifts and responsibilities to their team and debrief at morning
meetings. A physical sign in camp shows who leads what and who's on which
team — accountability is visible on purpose.

**It takes a minimum of 12 core members to activate at all.** Below that, the
build doesn't happen. Anyone past 12 makes the whole thing run better.

The collective is open to anyone contributing ideas or energy. Past, present,
and future members retain an equal voice during active deployments.

## Dues

Camp dues are collected per activation and calculated from the anticipated
costs of infrastructure, supplies, production, and logistics for that specific
event. Dues are divided equally among the people camping.

The collective has no group bank account. Dues are handled by the individuals
running that activation, and spending is tracked openly against a budget with
categories for production, infrastructure, and logistics.

**If you can't pay the full share in cash, say so.** If a member can't devote
enough time to planning and execution, the group may work out alternative
contributions — extra dues, expert services, equipment loans. It runs the
other way too: members who contribute significant time, resources, or
expertise may be eligible for reduced dues.

## What's expected of you

**Participate.** Setup, teardown, and daily operations. Attendance at camp
meetings and planning sessions is mandatory, and you're expected to bring
ideas, not just show up to them.

**Build.** Everyone helps put up and take down camp infrastructure — tents,
shade, communal areas. You may be assigned specific tasks based on your skills
and interests.

**Take your shift.** Shifts are assigned by Section Leads and posted where
everyone can see them. Accountability is visible on purpose.

**Communicate.** Openly and respectfully. Collaboration is the default and
supporting each other is the job.

---

## When things go wrong

Conflicts get addressed openly and constructively, through dialogue and
mediation.

Every voice matters, and resolving a problem matters more than avoiding an
uncomfortable conversation. Each member is responsible for raising concerns as
soon as it's practical, and for receiving feedback from others productively.
Camp leadership will help mediate and make sure everyone feels heard.

## Before every activation

Every member reviews and signs off on:

1. Camp Expectations and Responsibilities
2. Jellyfish Camp Safety Guidelines
3. Consent — including the "Tea and Consent" video

The attestation isn't a formality. If you haven't read them, read them.
