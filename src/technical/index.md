Every element, down to each individually hung jellyfish, is months of
planning by people who care far too much about it.

Here is what that actually means.

---

## Our theory

We came to participate, not to consume. It would be easier to buy a ticket,
watch somebody else's show, and go home. Showing up for us means adding
something to the event rather than drawing from it, and what we have to add
is our collective skill and a stupid amount of labor. That is the gift, and
giving it is the entire point. Nobody here is paid, nobody sells anything,
and everything we build is free to whoever wanders in.

Dancing is not enough. Anyone can point a speaker at a field. What people
actually want is to be taken somewhere, to step through a threshold and find
that the everyday world does not reach past it. We are not building a stage.
We are building a place that swallows you for a while and hands you back
changed.

The goal is disbelief. The reaction we are chasing is somebody stopping at
the edge of the tent and asking how on earth this got here. If nobody
wonders how we did it, we did not do enough.

We build it ourselves. Sound, light, structure, environment. None of it is
farmed out to vendors who have never seen the vision. We are DJs, artists,
sound engineers, lighting designers, and stubborn DIYers, which means
everything you see has been imagined by one jelly or another.

Everything has to come apart. If a piece cannot be broken down, packed flat,
and stored between festivals, we do not build it. Modularity is the
constraint that lets a volunteer crew put up something this ambitious in a
single day.

---

## What sets us apart

The seams line up, the cables are covered, the sound is tuned, and the thing
you walk into feels finished rather than improvised. Most camps this size
rent a stage or hire a production company. We are the production company,
and nobody gets paid. The whole thing is a gift, which is exactly why we
refuse to hand anyone a half built one.

The other half of it is people. Everyone here is unreasonably good at
something, and the whole model is built around finding that thing and
getting out of the way. The person who designs the lasers designs the
lasers. The person who knows how to talk to festival organizers talks to
festival organizers.

Because we own our infrastructure and document every build, each activation
starts from where the last one ended instead of from scratch. We survey
ourselves after every event, write down what broke, and fix it before the
next one.

Then we take it all down, pack it out, and leave nothing behind but the
photos.
