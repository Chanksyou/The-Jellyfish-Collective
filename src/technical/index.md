Burner culture runs on shared knowledge. Most of what we've figured out came
from other camps being generous with theirs, so here's ours.

---

## Footprint and timeline

We ask for a **60 x 60 minimum** footprint for the activation, though the
ideal is **120 x 60** — the larger space is what lets the open-field lounge
and the dancefloor coexist without either one crowding the other. Personal
camping stays away from the stage.

**One full day to build, half a day to strike**, including leave-no-trace.
Gear moves from our storage unit in a rented Sprinter van and gets staged
manually — no forklifts, no heavy power equipment, nothing that needs a
certified operator.

We're hands-on, organized, and self-reliant by design. Twelve core members is
the floor for activating at all.

## Structure

The lounge is built around a modular pole-and-fabric system — a large central
tent flanked by smaller structures, all of it designed to be raised by a crew
of a few people in an afternoon and struck just as fast.

Everything is chosen against three constraints, in this order: it has to be
simple to build, simple to break down, and simple to store. A structure that
looks incredible and takes nine hours to raise is a structure we will resent
by year three.

Practical lessons, learned the hard way:

- **Stake down your EZ-ups.** One of ours flipped because we didn't. It only
  has to happen once.
- **Label your bins before the event, not during.** Unlabeled totes mean
  everything is lost and nothing is findable at 2am.
- **Collect tent dimensions in advance.** Camp layout is a packing problem,
  and you can't solve it on-site with a tape measure and vibes.
- **Bring spare poles.** Always more than you think.

## Sound and light

Production gear comes through a longstanding partnership with Made to Order
NYC, a NYC event production collective that shares storage with us year-round.
They supply commercial-grade sound, lighting, lasers, haze, and projection for
our activations; we help carry the storage cost that keeps everything in one
place. The practical upshot is that we don't levy separate AV dues — sound
quality is not the thing we economize on.

The main system runs QSC tops and subs with additional stacks outside, driven
through a Pioneer controller and a dedicated speaker management console.
Lighting is a mix of laser, LED wallwash, and uplighting, timed to move with
the room rather than strobe at it.

**Shelly**, the art car, syncs directly to the main system so the sound stays
unified across the whole footprint instead of fighting itself at the edges.

## Power and electrical safety

This is the part where we're boring on purpose. Most camp emergencies are
electrical or fuel-related, and nearly all of them are preventable.

The electrical grid is managed by experienced engineers. Safety tape and trip
covers go in throughout camp. We can supply our own generator if the event
can't. And at least three **Safety Guardians** are on hand at all times to
monitor the activation and keep an eye on everyone's comfort.

**Generators**

- Sited at least 20 feet from any tent, stage, or enclosed space — carbon
  monoxide is the risk, and it's silent
- Properly grounded per manufacturer instructions
- Enclosures that ventilate while keeping hands out
- Sized with headroom for total connected load, never run at the edge

**Fuel**

- Approved, clearly labeled containers, stored cool and dry, away from
  ignition sources
- Refuel only when the generator is off and cooled, with a funnel, and clean
  spills immediately

**Cable**

- Outdoor-rated only, gauged for the actual load
- Ramps or covers anywhere cable crosses a walkway
- Routed away from water and sharp edges, secured so nothing tangles
- Inspected for fraying before every event; damaged cable gets replaced, not
  taped
- Weatherproof covers on every plug and outlet
- Unplug by the plug, never by yanking the cord

**Load management**

- Total connected wattage calculated in advance against generator capacity
- Load distributed evenly across circuits rather than stacked on one
- High-draw equipment powered on sequentially to avoid surge
- LED fixtures wherever possible — less heat, less draw

**People**

- Everyone gets basic electrical safety orientation and knows how to shut the
  generator down
- Clear signage around electrical equipment
- Only qualified people open anything up
- Battery or solar emergency lighting staged for a blackout

## Kitchen

Communal meals are part of the camp, and the kitchen is where the schedule
actually breaks if you let it.

- Prep food off-site wherever possible; on-site prep eats the whole afternoon
- Staff prep separately from the stove — whoever is cooking should not also be
  chopping
- Communal burners for people cooking their own food
- Three-bucket wash system for dishes, set up before anyone needs it
